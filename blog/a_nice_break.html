A nice break

Andy Tseng

Today marks a month since I have left Microsoft. I worked at Microsoft for about 4.8 years and worked on the same product since I arrived there. The main reasons I chose to quit was mostly financial and lack of interest in the product. Noticing the financial cliff that comes after working at a big tech company for 4 years, I chose to use this time to re-evaluate my interests and take a much needed break.

The events that happened in this year-to-date also contributed to my departure, but I would say it mostly tipped the financial scale. If I was paid more, I would have attempted to actually resolve the issues that I will be describing below.

For most of the 2026, I learned quite a bit about the industry and the realities of dealing with pressure from leadership. This pressure would trickle down to you in the form of your managers increasing expectations on your already large workload. During the holidays of 2025 after my previous manager left the company, I chose to spend more time working in proving my worth to my new manager. I will name this manager Charlie. During the month of December, it is common for people at Microsoft to take the whole month off. Usually I would fly back to my hometown and spend time with my parents. I would work as Christmas wasn't too big of a thing in my household.

During the last two weeks, there was an issue with how our application interacted with a specific plugin. The plugin was owned by another team in the company and they needed help with troubleshooting the issue. They were also adamant that we should be owning the bug. I did not have any experience nor knowledge of the interface in our application that works with plugins, but I was the only responsive person to the team. Alas I got looped into the discussions and troubleshooting. I helped the team with getting the telemetry data and spent a little time providing test builds.

While trying to provide test builds, I made silly mistakes in giving links that they did not have permissions and such. I would correct the links and re-provide them the versions. During this process, Charlie would attempt to coach me and tell me that he wants me to "do better" in terms of providing the links and be more responsive to the team.

This experience soured me, and I also noticed that Charlie did not quite understand how our application really worked. In their coaching feedback, they glossed over small details and told me to provide information that I provided from the start of the investigation. The overall tech stack did not seem clear to them.

Coming into 2026, our designated a set of people to tackle tech debt and bugs. I thought this was rather silly as I always thought high priority bugs should just get tackled and customer facing things get squashed ASAP. Any other bugs need to get scheduled and fixed accordingly. This began about 3 months of non-stop bug investigation and fixing.

Eventually Charlie became my skip manager and a new manager joined to replace my previous manager. I will name this manager Alpha. Alpha was a younger person and joined the company when there was a incident filed against our application. This incident was from a bug that I wrote in a security fix around the start of 2026. I could go into incredible detail in why this bug wasn't actually my mistake, but explaining this to Charlie and Alpha were all seens as excuses. I dealt with the issue swiftly, but this incident would be held over my head until the week I left Microsoft.

This incident alone blocked my promotion for that year and put me in a negative light for both Charlie and Alpha. Alpha took on all the impressions and learnings of how to manage me from Charlie and their impression on my stayed steadfast. Expectations were higher on me and my performance was seen as unacceptable despite working long hours to rescue it. I fixed myriads of bugs and even helped Alpha with his deliverables when they were stuck on any issues on it. By the end of May 2026, I felt like I was trapped with no way out of this.

Quickly my morale was dashed and going to work became a huge burden on my mental. I became very impatient with others and began whispering to myself about how much I disliked work whenever I was alone. A decently sized tenure that I built was slowly eroding. I knew I was going to leave the company by the end of the year, but the events still affected me quite a bit.

Very little work was getting done and I had no interest in the future work that was going to get assigned to me. I put in my two weeks at the start of August and basically no-showed for several days within my last weeks at the company. During this time, Charlie and Alpha would give a decent impression as they understood why I suddenly did not show up, but reflecting on it now; I wished I no showed the whole two weeks when I was not guaranteed by severance by Alpha.

This was a quick summary timeline of my 2026 so far. The choice to leave was 100% the correct decision and I am very happy the moment I did it. My time at Microsoft was interesting, and I will write a more detailed blog about the things I learned while working at this big tech company.
